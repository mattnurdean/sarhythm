Why Open Water Brightens (+1.66 dB) While Reedbeds Darken (−1.48 dB):
L-band radar responds oppositely depending on emergent vegetation:
• Open Water → Mudflat (e.g., Sector 19, +1.66 dB): Calm water acts as a dark mirror; when it dries, exposed mud scatters more energy back (brightens).
• Flooded Reeds → Dry Reeds (e.g., Sector 04, −1.48 dB): Standing water under vertical Phragmites stalks creates a bright double-bounce corner reflector; when the water drops below the roots, that bounce vanishes (darkens).
Raw L-Band Backscatter (dB): HH Co-Pol (Blue) & HV Cross-Pol (Purple)
Why Baseline Areas Differ While Change Matches (−6.98 vs −6.94 km²):
Both NISAR and NASA OPERA DSWx-HLS were evaluated over the exact same 90 km² Tiszavalki UTM box. Optical HLS (33.22 km²) classifies floating water-chestnut mats as land, whereas 24 cm L-band radar (43.83 km²) penetrates thin foliage to detect water underneath. Differencing August from June cancels that static vegetation offset (−6.98 km² vs −6.94 km²).
